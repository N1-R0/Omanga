# 90-day content roadmap (from 2026-10-28)

Method, per the owner's workflow: a real question from AnswerThePublic, an
interview with the owner, then the post. Two posts a week is the stated
target; this plan schedules what has evidence behind it. Every health or
money claim needs owner sign-off; health posts also need Phillips HMO review.

## Weeks 1–2 (before December travel peaks)

**1. Can I use my card in Nigeria? Revolut, Monzo, Wise and bank cards**
- Intent: informational, pre-trip. Cluster: "can i use my [brand] card in nigeria".
- Angle: what a visitor actually meets at the till in Lagos: card acceptance,
  POS charges, transfers as the default way to pay.
- Outline: Do foreign cards work? · ATMs and limits · Why sellers ask for a
  transfer · Paying by transfer without a Nigerian bank account · What to carry.
- Links: /payments, /rates/pounds-to-naira, /blog/how-far-does-100-dollars-go-in-nigeria, /safety.
- CTA: open a wallet. Evidence: owner interview; no claims about other
  providers' fees.

**2. Detty December 2026 refresh**
- Update dates, prices and events; check the "mid-market" wording decision (audit #11).

## Weeks 3–6

**3. Visiting Nigeria for the first time: a money and health checklist**

  Department advisories; Omanga makes no safety claims of its own.
- Links: /insurance, /payments, /blog/health-insurance-for-visiting-nigeria.

**4. Add "What does $20 buy in Nigeria?" to the $100 post**

## Not planned (and why)

- "Send money to Nigeria from the USA": dominated by Wise, Remitly, LemFi; not
  Omanga's core promise.
- "Dollar to naira black market": parallel-market rates are off-brand and a
  regulatory risk.
- "Travel insurance for Africa": the product covers Nigeria only.
